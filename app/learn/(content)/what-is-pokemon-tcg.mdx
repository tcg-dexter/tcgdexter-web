# What you're playing

If you've ever opened a pack of Pokémon cards and wondered what to actually *do* with them, start here.

The **Pokémon Trading Card Game** is a two-player battle game. Each player brings a 60-card deck and races to take **6 Prize cards**. You take a Prize card by knocking out one of your opponent's Pokémon. First to all six wins.

That's the whole game in three sentences. Everything after this is detail — and the detail is where it gets good.

## The table

Before any of the vocabulary lands, it helps to see where things go. Here's a real game, a few turns in. Your half is the near mat; your opponent's is the mirror image above it, and your two Active Pokémon face each other in the middle.

<Board />

Five things to notice, because every later lesson leans on them:

- Your **Active Pokémon** is the one doing the fighting. There is exactly one, always.
- Your **Bench** holds up to five more. They're safe from most attacks, and they're where you build up your next attacker.
- Your **Prize cards** are six cards from your own deck, set aside face down at the start. Taking them all is how you win — so your Prizes are also a countdown timer.
- Your **Discard pile** isn't a graveyard. A lot of cards pull things back out of it.
- The **Stadium** is shared. Only one can be in play across both players at once.

## A game, start to finish

1. Both players shuffle, draw 7, and set up a face-down Active and Bench.
2. Each player sets 6 Prize cards aside.
3. Players alternate turns: draw a card, play cards, attack.
4. When an attack leaves damage on a Pokémon equal to or above its HP, that Pokémon is **Knocked Out** and the attacker takes a Prize card.
5. First player to take all 6 Prizes wins.

Games run about 15–30 minutes. The rhythm clicks after two or three.

## Standard and Expanded

You'll see two **formats** mentioned:

- **Standard** is the current competitive format. Only recent sets are legal, and the pool rotates roughly once a year.
- **Expanded** uses a much larger pool going back further.

If you're new, you only need Standard. It's what tournaments use, what most decks are built for, and what Dexter checks against by default.
