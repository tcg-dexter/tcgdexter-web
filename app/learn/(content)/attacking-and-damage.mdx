# Attacking and damage

Attacking is the only way to take Prize cards, and it's the last thing you do on a turn. Here's exactly how one resolves.

## What you need to attack

Three things, all at once:

1. The Pokémon is in the **Active Spot**. Benched Pokémon never attack.
2. It has **the right Energy attached** to pay the attack's cost.
3. It isn't Asleep or Paralyzed (lesson 9).

<Card id="me5-28" />

Miraidon's Thunder costs two Lightning Energy and does 90. Two Lightning attached means you can use it. One Lightning and one Water means you can't — a typed symbol needs that type.

Colorless symbols are the exception: any Energy pays a Colorless cost.

<Callout kind="gotcha">
Attacking does **not** discard the Energy. It stays attached, and you can attack again next turn. Only an attack that explicitly says to discard Energy — like Miraidon's bigger cousins — actually costs you anything.
</Callout>

## Damage, counted in tens

Damage is always a multiple of 10, tracked with counters worth 10 each. It **stays on the Pokémon** across turns until something heals it.

A Pokémon is **Knocked Out** the moment damage on it is **equal to or greater than its HP**. Exactly equal counts — 120 damage on a 120 HP Pokémon is a Knock Out.

When that happens: the Pokémon, its Energy, and its Tools all go to the discard pile, and the attacking player takes their Prize card or cards.

## Weakness

<Callout kind="rule">
If the defending Pokémon's **Weakness** matches the attacking Pokémon's type, the damage is **doubled**.
</Callout>

Miraidon's Thunder does 90. Against a Pokémon Weak to Lightning, it does **180**. That's not a bonus — it's a different attack entirely, and it's why matchups swing so hard.

Weakness is checked against the **type of the attacking Pokémon**, not the type of Energy you paid with.

## Resistance

Resistance is the mirror image: it subtracts damage instead of doubling it. It's printed in the slot between Weakness and Retreat Cost.

<Callout kind="gotcha">
In current Standard you can safely ignore it. **No Standard-legal Pokémon prints a Resistance** — the slot is on the card layout but it's left empty. You'll only meet it in Expanded and older cards, where it typically reads −30.
</Callout>

## The order things apply

When an attack does several things at once, resolve it in this order:

1. Start with the **printed damage**.
2. Apply anything that **adds** damage (the attack's own text, Tools, Stadiums, Abilities).
3. Apply **Weakness** — double it.
4. Apply **Resistance** — subtract it.
5. Apply anything that **reduces** damage (defensive Tools, protective Abilities).

Getting the order wrong matters. Weakness doubles the *boosted* number, not the printed one — a +30 effect on a 90 attack against Weakness is (90+30) × 2 = 240, not 90 × 2 + 30 = 210.

## Healing and moving damage

Some cards heal — remove damage counters — and some move damage from one Pokémon to another. Both matter more than they look.

A Pokémon healed back to full is a Pokémon your opponent has to spend *another* two turns knocking out, while your Prize count hasn't moved. That's why "just heal it" decks are genuinely competitive even though they never seem to be doing anything.
