# What makes a legal deck

Before you can play a deck, it has to be legal. There are only four rules, and every one of them is checkable in about ten seconds.

This lesson is about the rules. *Which* 60 cards to pick is a much bigger topic — that's what the rest of Dexter is for.

## 1. Exactly 60 cards

Not 59, not 61. **Exactly 60.**

Every card counts: Pokémon, Trainers and Energy all come out of the same 60. There's no separate Energy pile the way some other card games do it.

## 2. At most 4 copies of any card name

<Callout kind="rule">
Four copies maximum, counted **by card name**, not by printing.
</Callout>

That second part catches people out. If Ultra Ball has been printed in six different sets, you still get four Ultra Balls total — not four of each. Different art, different set code, same name, same limit.

## 3. Basic Energy is unlimited

The one exception to the 4-copy rule. Run as many Basic Energy cards as you want.

Special Energy is *not* exempt — it has a name and an effect, so it's capped at 4 like everything else.

## 4. One ACE SPEC card, total

Not one per name. **One ACE SPEC in the whole deck.** Pick your single most powerful card and that's your allowance spent.

## Standard legality and regulation marks

Those four rules make a deck *valid*. Playing in Standard adds one more: every card has to still be in the format.

Look at the bottom-left of any recent card and you'll find a small letter — the **regulation mark**. Once a year, the oldest marks rotate out and stop being Standard-legal. Everything with a current mark stays.

Two wrinkles worth knowing:

- **Basic Energy never rotates.** It has no regulation mark and it's always legal.
- **Trainer cards go by name, not printing.** If a Trainer has been reprinted with a current mark, your older copy of that same card is legal too — even though the mark on it has rotated. This does *not* apply to Pokémon, whose different printings have genuinely different text.
