## Stages, and how a Pokémon enters play

- **Basic** — play it from your hand straight onto your Bench.
- **Stage 1** — play it on top of the matching Basic already in play.
- **Stage 2** — play it on top of the matching Stage 1.

You can't skip a step by playing a Stage 2 onto a Basic. (One Item card, Rare Candy, does let you — more on that in lesson 7.)

<Callout kind="rule">
Evolving isn't optional and it isn't automatic — it's something you choose to do on your turn, and each Pokémon can evolve **once per turn**.
</Callout>

## Rule boxes: ex and Mega

Some Pokémon carry an extra box of text at the bottom. That box is a warning label.

<Card id="sv8-57" />

Pikachu ex hits for 300. The rule box is the price: **when a Pokémon ex is Knocked Out, your opponent takes 2 Prize cards** instead of 1. A **Mega** Pokémon — a Pokémon ex that has Mega Evolved — gives up **3**.

So a deck full of ex Pokémon is a deck where your opponent only needs three good turns. That trade-off drives most deckbuilding decisions you'll ever make.

## Damage stays put

When a Pokémon takes damage, that damage **stays on it** between turns. It doesn't heal at end of turn. Players track it with damage counters — small markers worth 10 each — or with dice.

So a Pokémon with 120 HP that took 90 last turn is sitting one small attack away from a Knock Out. Reading the board means reading the damage already on it, not just the printed HP.
