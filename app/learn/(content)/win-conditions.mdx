# Knockouts and how you win

There are exactly **three ways** to win. Nearly every game ends by the first, but the other two are real and knowing them changes how you play.

## 1. Take all six of your Prize cards

Knock out an opponent's Pokémon, take a Prize card. Take all six, win.

Not every Knock Out pays the same:

| Knocked Out | Prizes taken |
|---|---|
| A normal Pokémon | **1** |
| A Pokémon **ex** | **2** |
| A **Mega** Pokémon | **3** |

So one Knock Out on an ex is worth two on anything else. That arithmetic is the game.

## 2. Your opponent has no Pokémon in play

If their Active is Knocked Out and their Bench is empty, the game ends instantly — there's nothing to promote.

It's rare in long games, but it's a genuine early-game angle. An opponent who opened with a single Basic and hasn't drawn another is one Knock Out from losing, whatever the Prize count says.

## 3. Your opponent can't draw a card

Drawing is the first thing you do on a turn. If a player's deck is empty when their turn begins, they **lose immediately**.

Most decks flirt with this in long games. A few are built around it deliberately, forcing the opponent to burn through cards until there are none left.

## You're racing, not clearing the board

The most important idea in this lesson: **you're racing to six Prizes, not trying to knock out everything they have.** You never need to clear their board. You need six well-chosen Knock Outs before they get six.

Which makes *target selection* the highest-leverage skill in the game.

## Picking the right target

Once your attacker is ready, the question isn't "can I Knock this Out?" — it's "which Knock Out actually moves me closer to winning?" Five things to weigh:

### 1. Your Prize count

Your remaining Prizes are the clock. At 2 Prizes, taking out one Mega wins on the spot. At 6, the same attack is just a start. Read **both** Prize stacks every turn — the right play at 6 is often the wrong play at 2.

### 2. What's on their Bench

If their Active is all they have, Knocking it out **wins the game**. Even with a Bench, what's on it matters: a benched ex is 2 Prizes you can chase later, and a benched setup Pokémon might be the engine keeping their whole hand working.

### 3. Energy and Tools on the Active

A loaded Active — three Energy and a Tool — is turns of investment. Knock it out and all of it hits the discard. Knock out an empty support Pokémon instead and you've traded a Prize for none of their resources.

### 4. Abilities on the board

Some Pokémon are dangerous without ever attacking. Abilities only work while the Pokémon is in play, so removing a 1-Prize support piece can switch off their entire engine — worth far more than the single Prize card suggests.

### 5. What you give back

Your attacker is a target too. Spending a 3-Prize Mega to take a 1-Prize Knock Out is a losing trade: they swing back at something worth three times what you took. Save the big hitters for big targets.

## Single-prize decks

This is why decks built entirely from non-ex attackers stay competitive even when ex Pokémon hit harder card-for-card.

If every Pokémon you put down is worth 1 Prize, your opponent needs **six** Knock Outs to win. Meanwhile you're taking their ex for 2 and their Megas for 3. You can lose the damage race comfortably and still win the Prize race.

## Where this goes next

You're not just picking strong cards — you're picking attackers whose Prize math works against the decks you'll actually face.
