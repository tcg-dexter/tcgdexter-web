# How a turn works

A turn has a strict beginning and end, and a lot of freedom in the middle. Once the shape is familiar, you'll start seeing a turn as a budget — and playing well is mostly about spending it well.

## The fixed parts

1. **Draw a card.** Always first. If your deck is empty when you have to draw, you lose on the spot.
2. **Do things.** The flexible middle — everything below.
3. **Attack.** Optional, and it *ends your turn* the moment you do it. You can also just pass.

## The flexible middle

In any order, as often as the limits allow:

- Play **Basic Pokémon** onto your Bench — as many as you like, up to 5 on the Bench
- **Evolve** your Pokémon — each one once per turn
- **Attach one Energy** from your hand, to any Pokémon
- Play **Item** cards — unlimited
- Play **one Supporter**
- Play **one Stadium**
- Attach **Tools** — unlimited, but one per Pokémon
- Use **Abilities** — usually once per turn each
- **Retreat** your Active Pokémon — once per turn

The hard caps are worth memorising, because they're the whole shape of a turn:

<Callout kind="rule">
One Energy attachment. One Supporter. One Stadium. One retreat. One attack.
Everything else is unlimited.
</Callout>

## Evolution timing

Evolving is the rule that trips up new players most, so here it is precisely:

- A Pokémon **cannot evolve the turn you play it.** It has to have been in play since the start of your turn.
- No Pokémon can evolve **on your very first turn of the game** — for the same reason: everything came down during setup.
- Each Pokémon can evolve **once per turn**. Basic → Stage 1 this turn, Stage 1 → Stage 2 next turn.
- Evolving **removes all Special Conditions** from that Pokémon. Damage stays; conditions don't.

So Gastly comes down on turn one. On turn two it can become Haunter. On turn three, Gengar. Three turns to get a Stage 2 online — unless you shortcut it:

<Card id="me1-125" />

Rare Candy takes a Basic already in play and evolves it **straight to Stage 2**, skipping the middle. It's an Item, so it doesn't use your Supporter, and it's the reason Stage 2 decks are playable at all.

## A worked turn

You have Gastly Active from last turn, one Bench slot filled, and a hand with options.

1. **Draw** — you get Rare Candy.
2. Play **Ultra Ball**, discarding 2 cards, to search out Gengar.
3. Play **Rare Candy** on Gastly → it becomes **Gengar** immediately, skipping Haunter.
4. Play **Cheren** to draw 3. That's your Supporter for the turn.
5. **Attach a Darkness Energy** to Gengar. That's your attachment for the turn.
6. **Attack.** Turn over.

Two Items, one Supporter, one attachment, one attack. Every cap respected, and everything uncapped stacked as high as the hand allowed.

<Callout kind="gotcha">
Notice the order. Cheren was played *after* the searching, not before — once you draw into a turn you can't undo it, so spend the cards you already know about before you go looking for new ones.
</Callout>

## Retreating

Once per turn you may retreat your Active Pokémon: discard Energy from it equal to its Retreat Cost, then swap it with a Benched Pokémon.

Two things fall out of that:

- The Energy is **gone**, not moved. Retreating a loaded attacker is expensive.
- Retreating **clears all Special Conditions**, because the Pokémon leaves the Active Spot. That's often the real reason to do it.
